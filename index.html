What is EXIF data?

Every photo from a phone or camera carries a hidden block of text called EXIF. It records the camera and lens, aperture, shutter speed and ISO, the date and time, the image size, and often the exact GPS coordinates of where you were standing. It is handy for photographers and risky if you share a photo of your home.

This viewer reads that block, plus XMP, IPTC and embedded colour profiles, and flags the parts that could be sensitive. When you are ready to act on it, use the other tools to remove location data, edit EXIF fields, copy EXIF between photos or export it.

Questions

Is it safe to use with private photos?

Yes. The viewer and editor work locally in your browser, so you can inspect or change metadata without sending images to a server. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and everything keeps working.

Which formats are supported?

You can view metadata in JPEG, PNG, WebP, TIFF, HEIC and AVIF files. Editing, removing and copying work on JPEG, PNG and WebP.

Why does my photo show no EXIF data?

Many websites, messaging apps and social networks remove metadata when you upload a picture, and screenshots never have camera data. If you saved the photo from a website, the original may have been stripped before it reached you.

Does a phone photo really contain my location?

If location services are on for your camera app, yes: the latitude and longitude are written into the file. The viewer shows it and offers a one-click way to remove it.
